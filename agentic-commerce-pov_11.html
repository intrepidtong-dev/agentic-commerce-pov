Point of View · Southeast Asia · April 2026

The last satisfying click is no click at all.

How agentic commerce will reshape Southeast Asia’s digital economy — and why the definition of “agentic” must stretch to capture the real opportunity.

Confidential · Internal Distribution
01 · Thesis

The region that invented conversational commerce is the region that will retire the click.

Southeast Asia is not a late adopter of agentic commerce. It is the most fertile ground for it, for reasons structural to how the region already shops.

Southeast Asia’s digital economy is on track to surpass USD 300 billion in GMV in 2025, with platform e-commerce alone reaching USD 157.6 billion — a 23% year-on-year jump — and now concentrated in three players that command 99% of platform market share: Shopee, Lazada, and TikTok Shop.1,2 This is the most concentrated major e-commerce market in the world, the most fragmented checkout experience for consumers, and the most promo-intensive shopping culture anywhere.

Against this backdrop, a specific prediction from Gartner reframes the next five years: by 2028, 60% of brands will use agentic AI to facilitate streamlined one-to-one interactions, ending channel-based marketing as we know it.3 Separately, Gartner’s 2026 outlook forecasts that 40% of enterprise applications will be integrated with task-specific AI agents by the end of 2026, up from less than 5% a year earlier.4 Agentic commerce is not on a distant horizon. It is the commerce architecture being built right now — and SEA will be where the most interesting experiments happen first.

The brands that win in SEA will not be the ones with the best app. They will be the ones whose AI agents eliminate the need for an app at all.
Central Thesis

But here is the complication most POVs gloss over: if we define agentic commerce narrowly — as fully autonomous agents executing end-to-end transactions without human intervention — the market in SEA is too small, too early, and too experimental to support a dedicated practice. If we define it expansively — to include the full spectrum of agentic AI applied to commerce outcomes, including content production at scale, creative decisioning, and agent-assisted customer engagement — we unlock a market that is already commercially viable and where Accenture Song has structural advantages no competitor can match.

This POV argues for the second definition, and builds the case for why Song’s unique position across marketing, social commerce, and commerce execution makes us the only firm in SEA positioned to win it.

02 · Definition

Agentic commerce is a spectrum, not a threshold.

The market gets stuck arguing about whether something is “truly agentic.” The more useful question is where a capability sits on two axes — autonomy and commerce impact — and how rapidly it is moving along them.

The two-axis definition

Rather than drawing a line between “agentic” and “not agentic,” we plot capabilities on a spectrum defined by two dimensions that together determine commercial value.

Axis 1 — Autonomy

The degree to which the AI system acts without human confirmation. At the low end: suggestion engines and copilots that require a human to click. At the high end: fully autonomous agents that execute multi-step transactions, negotiate, and escalate only at defined exception thresholds.

Axis 2 — Commerce Impact

The proximity of the AI capability to a commercial outcome. At the low end: internal productivity tools and operational efficiency. At the high end: direct influence on transaction, AOV, conversion, or cost-to-serve — with measurable P&L consequences.

Zone-by-zone: what qualifies, and why it matters

Zone 1 · Core Agentic
Autonomous commercial execution

AI systems that execute multi-step commercial actions with real-world consequences — no human click at the final step. These are not assistants; they are operators. The system receives intent, decomposes it, negotiates or optimises, and transacts.

Examples that qualify: Autonomous replenishment agents for grocery (FairPrice-pattern). Dynamic pricing and promotion agents. Payment orchestration agents that select optimal rails. B2B procurement agents that run RFQs end-to-end. Agent-led booking and dynamic upsell in hospitality.
Zone 2 · Adjacent Agentic
Agent-assisted decisioning at commerce scale

AI systems that operate alongside humans but take meaningful autonomous action within a constrained commerce context. Human defines the envelope; agent operates inside it. The commercial impact is direct, but a human remains in the loop for strategic overrides.

Examples that qualify: Personalisation engines that autonomously assemble offers. Live-commerce creative agents that generate, test, and optimise streams in real time. Shopper marketing optimisation that reallocates spend across channels without human approval at each step. Customer service agents that resolve service issues including refunds and credits.
Zone 3 · Agentic-Enabled
Generative content & creative ops for commerce

AI-driven content production at commerce scale, where agents generate, adapt, localise, and deploy creative assets that directly feed commerce outcomes. Autonomy is high within the content production pipeline; commerce impact is indirect but measurable (conversion lift, AOV, engagement).

Examples that qualify: Generative product merchandising at SKU scale (millions of PDPs, variants, localisations). Autonomous creative asset production for paid media feeding commerce. Social commerce creative automation (short-form video, UGC-style content) at platform scale. AI-driven catalogue localisation across SEA markets.
Why this definition matters commercially
Accenture is already the largest global producer of AI-driven content for commerce at scale. Excluding content production from our agentic commerce definition would cede our single strongest proof point — and the revenue base that anchors a viable practice — to a narrow technical purity argument. The spectrum definition lets us speak credibly about Core Agentic to analysts and CDOs, while building the practice on a revenue base that already exists.
03 · Why SEA, Why Now

The structural conditions that make SEA the world’s fastest-moving agentic commerce laboratory.

Five conditions are specific to Southeast Asia and collectively make the region move faster on agentic commerce than any other major market.

1. Extreme platform concentration meets consumer fragmentation

Three platforms — Shopee (52% regional share), TikTok Shop (including Tokopedia, ~28%), and Lazada (~15%) — now control 99% of Southeast Asia’s platform e-commerce GMV.2,5 Yet consumers toggle across all three plus WhatsApp, brand D2C, and live commerce in a single purchase journey. This fragmentation-at-the-consumer-level, concentration-at-the-platform-level dynamic is the exact condition agents are built to arbitrage.

2. Conversational commerce is the default, not the exception

Video and live commerce already generate USD 49.7 billion in GMV — 32% of SEA platform GMV.5 WhatsApp-based selling is dominant in Indonesia, Thailand, and the Philippines. Consumers are already comfortable transacting through conversation — the leap from human seller to AI agent is a modality shift, not a behaviour change. SEA has done the cultural work that Western markets are still doing.

3. AI adoption is already at consumer scale

According to the 2025 e-Conomy SEA report, 68% of SEA consumers are influenced by AI-generated recommendations.1 This is not a future state. Consumers already trust AI outputs in commerce decisions — making the transition to agents acting on their behalf materially shorter than in markets where AI is still viewed with skepticism.

4. Promo and deal-seeking behaviour is unmatched

SEA’s promotional intensity — 9.9, 11.11, 12.12, daily flash sales, voucher stacking, cross-platform price comparison — creates an optimisation problem no human shopper can solve efficiently. This is the ideal use case for Core Agentic systems: promo arbitrage, optimal timing, bundle construction, automated price discovery at speeds and accuracy no human can match.

5. SME merchant operational pain is acute and fragmented

Brands contribute less than 30% of SEA’s platform e-commerce GMV, compared to over 50% in China.6 The remainder is a long tail of SME sellers operating with minimal tooling: manual catalog updates, manual pricing, manual campaign participation. Agents acting as autonomous merchant operators represent a step-change for this segment and the single largest untapped market on the supply side.

Regional Context
SEA’s digital decade delivered. The AI decade will be faster.

GMV grew 7.4x over the past decade (from USD 40bn to USD 300bn+). Revenue grew 11.2x (forecast to hit USD 135bn in 2025).1 The next wave of growth in SEA — per the report’s framing — will be AI-led and monetisation-focused, not user-acquisition-led. Agents are the monetisation mechanism.

The macro case

McKinsey Global Institute estimates generative AI could deliver USD 2.6–4.4 trillion in annual global value, with approximately 75% concentrated in four functional areas: customer operations, marketing and sales, software engineering, and R&D.7 Marketing alone could see productivity gains of 5–15% of total marketing spend — approximately USD 463 billion annually.8 The commerce-adjacent zones (marketing, sales, customer ops) account for the majority of this value.

Separately, Gartner projects that AI agents will command USD 15 trillion in B2B purchases by 2028,9 and that agentic AI could drive approximately 30% of enterprise application software revenue by 2035, surpassing USD 450 billion.4 These are not marginal numbers.

The new revenue architecture: agent-as-a-channel

Beyond efficiency, agentic commerce opens a new monetisation layer for platforms and brands: sponsored placement within agent recommendation flows, commission on agent-executed transactions, and API access for third-party agents. Gartner already frames this as the emergence of “machine customers” with economic agency, driving demand for programmable financial infrastructure.10

05 · Song’s Position

The intersection that only Accenture Song can occupy.

Agentic commerce is not a single offering. It is the convergence of three capabilities we already operate at scale — and that no competitor in SEA holds together.

Three practices, one compounding advantage
Marketing + Social Commerce + Agentic Commerce — each strong on its own, exponentially stronger together.

Marketing

Demand generation, creative production, media, personalisation at scale. The layer that captures intent and shapes preference.

Creator engagement, live commerce enablement, community-led selling, short-form video production at platform scale.

The compounding effect

Marketing generates the intent signals that agentic systems consume. Social commerce is the primary surface on which SEA consumers already engage in conversational and video-first commerce — the exact modality agents will inhabit. Agentic commerce is the execution layer that turns marketing intent and social engagement into transactions, bundled offers, and retention loops. A competitor who owns only one of these three cannot replicate what we can deliver across all three.

Why no one else spans the intersection

Strategy firms (MBB) theorise about commerce transformation and agent operating models but have no production build-or-run capability. Platform implementers (Publicis Sapient, Deloitte Digital) own commerce platform rails but lack AI-native interaction design and creative production at scale. Creative and media agencies (WPP, Dentsu) design experiences for humans but have no control over transaction layers or enterprise systems. Hyperscalers (AWS, Google, Microsoft) provide infrastructure but cannot deliver the integrated advisory, design, and execution work an enterprise client needs.

Only Accenture Song operates at scale across: (a) strategy and design for intent and trust architectures, (b) AI decisioning through the Accenture AI and GenAI CoE, (c) production operations via SynOps for exception handling and governance, (d) enterprise execution rails through Salesforce Commerce Cloud, SAP, VTEX, and Adobe, (e) creative and content production at the volumes commerce requires, and (f) social and live commerce enablement as an already-funded practice area.

The strategic claim
We are not assembling these capabilities for agentic commerce. They are already assembled. What we are doing is recognising the intersection we already occupy and naming it as a coherent practice area. This is not a build. It is a reframe of capabilities already earning revenue.
06 · GTM Implications

What this POV commits our go-to-market strategy to.

A POV this expansive demands a GTM that explicitly connects Marketing, Social Commerce, and Agentic Commerce — not three parallel practices, but one coordinated go-to-market motion.

Three principles the GTM must honour

Principle 1
Offerings must span the full spectrum, not just Core Agentic.

The offering portfolio needs a clear entry point in each zone: Agentic-Enabled (content and creative at commerce scale), Adjacent Agentic (agent-assisted decisioning), and Core Agentic (autonomous execution). Clients will enter where their maturity allows and move along the spectrum. Forcing all entry through Core Agentic narrows the pipeline to the smallest segment of the market.

Principle 2
The Marketing and Social Commerce practices are the front door, not the adjacent business.

Clients currently buying AI-driven content production at scale from Song are the highest-probability buyers of Adjacent and Core Agentic offerings. The GTM should explicitly position content-for-commerce engagements as the on-ramp to a broader agentic roadmap — not as a separate revenue line. Marketing and Social Commerce sellers need to be equipped with the agentic narrative, not handed over to a separate practice.

Principle 3
The thought leadership narrative must be coherent across all three practice areas.

A CDO reading our agentic commerce POV, our social commerce POV, and our marketing transformation POV should see a single story, not three. The flagship SEA Agentic Commerce Report needs to explicitly position Marketing + Social Commerce + Agentic Commerce as the integrated capability stack, with each offering feeding the others. This is our differentiation. We should say it loudly.

Implications for beachhead accounts

Each beachhead account carries a different entry point on the spectrum, and the GTM should lead with the zone closest to where the client is already engaged with Song:

  • FairPrice Group — Lead with Core Agentic (replenishment) but anchor on Agentic-Enabled (content and merchandising at SKU scale) if that is where the existing relationship sits. Expand into Adjacent Agentic (promotions optimisation) as confidence builds.
  • Ascott — Lead with Adjacent Agentic (personalised booking and dynamic upsell), which has the fastest path to measurable revenue uplift. Use Agentic-Enabled (localised creative for booking flows) as the warm-up, and move to Core Agentic (full booking automation) once baselines are established.
  • Mastercard — Lead with Core Agentic (payment orchestration) because the value case is sharpest there, but position the Agentic-Enabled work (content for merchant acquisition, B2B marketing) as the multi-year revenue anchor that compounds across the network.
07 · Next Steps

Alignment on thesis, then alignment on money.

This POV is the foundation for the GTM deck that follows. Before the GTM is finalised, we need explicit alignment on three questions:

  1. 1. Are we comfortable with the spectrum definition? If yes, the practice revenue base is already in the hundreds of millions regionally. If no, we build a narrower, purer practice with a smaller near-term ceiling and a longer path to scale.
  2. 2. How explicitly do we integrate with Marketing and Social Commerce GTM? A shared pipeline view, shared account planning, and shared thought leadership calendar all follow from the answer. The less integrated, the more we cannibalise ourselves.
  3. 3. Which beachhead lands first? All three are winnable. Sequencing matters because the first live reference shapes the narrative we can credibly tell the next ten accounts. A Core Agentic win tells a different story than an Agentic-Enabled win. Both are valuable; we need to be deliberate about which we want to lead with.
